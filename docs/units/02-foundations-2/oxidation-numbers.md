## Vocabulary

These four terms come up in every redox problem.

**Oxidation**
:   Loss of e⁻ (also loss of H or gain of O).

**Reduction**
:   Gain of e⁻ (also gain of H or loss of O).

**Oxidation #** (oxidation number)
:   The charge on an atom in a compound. In these notes **#X** means the oxidation number of X,
    so #Na is the oxidation number of sodium.

**Redox Reaction** (Oxidation-Reduction Reaction)
:   A reaction in which one substance is oxidized and another is reduced.

These concepts are the foundation of Electrochemistry. {.star}

Dimensional Analysis is required in the AP Test! {.star}

## The rules

Four rules let you work out the oxidation number of any element in a compound.

| Rule | Statement | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Rule #1** | Group A oxidation numbers are equal to the charges. | $\ce{Na^{1+}}$ and $\ce{S^2-}$ |
| **Rule #2** | Atoms as elements or molecules have an oxidation number of zero. | <span class="boxed">Ca</span> : 0 and <span class="boxed">S</span> : 0 |
| **Rule #3** | In a neutral compound, the sum of all oxidation numbers is equal to zero. | NaCl: $\#\text{Na} + \#\text{Cl} = 1 - 1 = 0$ |
| **Rule #4** | In a polyatomic ion, the sum of all oxidation numbers equals the charge of the ion. | $\ce{PO4^3-}$: $\#\text{P} + 4\cdot\#\text{O} = -3$ |

!!! formula "Oxidation number of an element"
    $$\#X = 0 \quad \text{(atoms as elements or molecules)}$$

!!! formula "Oxidation number sum (neutral compound)"
    $$\sum \#X = 0$$

!!! formula "Oxidation number sum (polyatomic ion)"
    $$\sum \#X = \text{charge of the ion}$$

## Hydrogen, oxygen and halogens

These elements change oxidation number depending on what they are bonded to and where they sit
in the formula.

### Hydrogen

!!! formula "Oxidation number of hydrogen"
    | Situation | #H | Example |
    |---|---|---|
    | Molecule ($\ce{H2}$) | 0 | — |
    | Atom (H) | 0 | — |
    | First element in a compound | +1 | $\ce{H2SO4}$ |
    | Second element in a compound, called **hydride** | −1 | $\ce{NaH}$, $\ce{CaH2}$ |

### Oxygen

!!! formula "Oxidation number of oxygen"
    | Situation | #O | Example |
    |---|---|---|
    | Molecule ($\ce{O2}$) | 0 | — |
    | Atom (O) | 0 | — |
    | Oxide, where the formula of an ionic compound is valid | −2 | $\ce{Na+} + \ce{O^2-} = \ce{Na2O}$ |
    | Peroxide ($\ce{O2}$ = −2) | −1 | $\ce{Na2O2}$ |

!!! ap "AP Favorite!"
    **Peroxides** violate the ionic compound rules: the two oxygens stay bonded together as
    $[\ce{O-O}]^{2-} = \ce{(O2)^2-}$, so each O is −1. For example,
    $\ce{Na^{+1}} + \ce{(O2)^{-2}} = \ce{Na2O2}$.

### Halogens

**Halogens** are group 7A (17) and are usually shown as X.

!!! formula "Oxidation number of halogens"
    | Situation | #X | Example |
    |---|---|---|
    | Molecule ($\ce{X2}$) | 0 | — |
    | Atom (X) | 0 | — |
    | Second element in a compound | −1 | $\ce{Ca^2+} + \ce{Cl-} = \ce{CaCl2}$ |
    | First element in a compound | Some positive charge that has to be derived | $\ce{Cl2O7}$ (worked below) |

## Worked examples

To derive an oxidation number, set the sum of all oxidation numbers equal to the overall charge
(Rules #3 and #4) and solve.

!!! example "Example: Find the oxidation number of Cl in Cl₂O₇"
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2\cdot\#\text{Cl} + 7\cdot\#\text{O} &= 0\\
    2\cdot\#\text{Cl} + 7(-2) &= 0\\
    2\cdot\#\text{Cl} &= 14\\
    \#\text{Cl} &= +7
    \end{aligned}
    $$

!!! example "Example: Find the oxidation number of Cr from K₂Cr₂O₇"
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2\cdot\#\text{Cr} + 2\cdot\#\text{K} + 7\cdot\#\text{O} &= 0\\
    2\cdot\#\text{Cr} + 2(1) + 7(-2) &= 0\\
    2\cdot\#\text{Cr} &= 12\\
    \#\text{Cr} &= +6
    \end{aligned}
    $$

!!! example "Example: Find the oxidation number of P in PO₄³⁻"
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \#\text{P} + 4\cdot\#\text{O} &= -3\\
    \#\text{P} + 4(-2) &= -3\\
    \#\text{P} + (-8) &= -3\\
    \#\text{P} &= +5
    \end{aligned}
    $$

## Oxidation vs. reduction

Oxidation and reduction are opposites. They can be described by hydrogen, by oxygen, or by
electrons.

| | Oxidation | Reduction |
|---|---|---|
| **H** | Loss of H | Gain of H |
| **O** | Gain of O | Loss of O |
| ☆ **e⁻** | Loss of e⁻: increases (+) charge, decreases (−) charge | Gain of e⁻: decreases (+) charge, increases (−) charge |

The e⁻ row is the definition used on the AP. {.star}

## Oxidizing & reducing agents

Each agent is named for what it does to the other substance.

**Reducing Agent** (Reducer)
:   The compound being **oxidized**.

**Oxidizing Agent** (Oxidizer)
:   The compound being **reduced**.

!!! example "Example: Find the oxidation number of Mn"
    $$
    \begin{array}{ccc}
    \ce{KMnO4} & \xrightarrow{\qquad\qquad} & \ce{MnO2}\\[6pt]
    1 + \#\text{Mn} + 4(-2) = 0 & & \#\text{Mn} + 2(-2) = 0\\
    -7 + \#\text{Mn} = 0 & & \#\text{Mn} = +4\\
    \#\text{Mn} = +7 & &
    \end{array}
    $$

    $\ce{Mn^7+}$ is reduced to $\ce{Mn^4+}$, ∴ $\ce{KMnO4}$ is the **oxidizing agent** (Oxidizer).
    Going the other way, $\ce{Mn^4+}$ would be oxidized to $\ce{Mn^7+}$, ∴ it would be the
    **reducing agent** (Reducer).
