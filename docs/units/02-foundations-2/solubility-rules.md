## Charts to memorize

Solubility rules tell you which products of a reaction stay dissolved and which form a solid, so
you know which ions to keep in the net ionic equation. Memorize both charts.

Net ionic equations are based on *memorizing* the solubility chart! {.star}

## Always soluble

**Nitrates, Acetates, Ammonium and Group 1A** compounds are always soluble.

## Using the charts

To decide if a compound is soluble, find its anion in one of the charts, then check whether its
cation is in the *Except* column. In Chart I an exception makes it **insoluble**; in Chart II an
exception makes it **soluble**.

| Compound | Look at | Soluble? |
|---|---|---|
| $\ce{Ca(NO3)2}$, $\ce{Al(NO3)3}$ | $\ce{(NO3)-}$: always soluble | Soluble |
| $\ce{Pb(CH3COO)2}$ | $\ce{(CH3COO)-}$: always soluble | Soluble |
| $\ce{AgCl}$ | $\ce{Cl-}$ with $\ce{Ag+}$ (Chart I exception) | **Insoluble** |
| $\ce{PbI2}$ | $\ce{I-}$ with $\ce{Pb^2+}$ (Chart I exception) | **Insoluble** |
| $\ce{BaSO4}$ | $\ce{(SO4)^2-}$ with heavy metal $\ce{Ba^2+}$ (Chart I exception) | **Insoluble** |
| $\ce{K2SO4}$ | $\ce{(SO4)^2-}$ with Group 1A | Soluble |
| $\ce{CaCO3}$ | $\ce{(CO3)^2-}$, $\ce{Ca^2+}$ is not an exception | **Insoluble** |
| $\ce{(NH4)3PO4}$ | $\ce{(PO4)^3-}$ with $\ce{(NH4)+}$ (Chart II exception) | Soluble |
| $\ce{Ba(OH)2}$ | $\ce{(OH)-}$ with heavy metal (Chart II exception) | Soluble (a strong base) |
| $\ce{Fe(OH)3}$ | $\ce{(OH)-}$, $\ce{Fe^3+}$ is not an exception | **Insoluble** |
| $\ce{Na2S}$ | $\ce{S^2-}$ with Group 1A (Chart II exception) | Soluble |

An insoluble product is the **precipitate** (s) in a net ionic equation. See the worked
precipitation examples in [Net Ionic Equations](net-ionic-equations-titration.md#net-ionic-equation-precipitation).

## Heavy metals

**Heavy metals**
:   Ca, Sr and Ba. Their hydroxides are strong bases.

## Latin names (-ous / -ic)

Older Latin names for metal ions use **-ous** for the lower charge and **-ic** for the higher
charge. The same endings are used to name oxyacids.
