Type a chemical formula: element symbols with a capital first letter, numbers for subscripts,
parentheses for groups, and a dot for hydrates. The calculator uses the atomic masses printed on
the [AP periodic table](reference.md), so the answers match the ones you'd get on the exam.

<!-- wiki:molar-mass -->

## How it works

Each element's count is multiplied by its atomic mass, and the results are added together. A
subscript after parentheses multiplies everything inside them: $\ce{Ca(NO3)2}$ has 1 Ca, 2 N and
6 O. The converter below the result follows the same
[conversion chain](units/01-foundations-1/stoichiometry.md#conversion-chain) as the notes:
grams ⇄ moles ⇄ particles, with the units carried through every step.
